# Lower bounds on the sheltered spin and exposure rate

## Three exposed spins are inversion-blind

Expand an invariant state and an invariant effect in real Pauli tensors, grouping by the exact nonidentity support $`J\subseteq\{1,2,3\}`$. A local rotation preserves each support. The invariant tensor space on the empty support is the scalars. It is zero on any singleton and one-dimensional on each pair, generated by the Kronecker delta. It is one-dimensional on the triple, generated by the Levi-Civita tensor. These dimensions follow from the spin-1 representation, with a unique scalar in its tensor square and a unique copy of spin 1 in its tensor square to couple with the third factor.

On every nonzero invariant support, the effect and state each have a coefficient times the same tensor $v_J$. Under the local rotations the probability contribution is proportional to

```math
v_J^{\mathsf T}\!\left(\bigotimes_{i\in J}R_i\right)v_J,
```

where $R_i$ is the real adjoint rotation of $U_i$. Field reversal transposes the tensor product. The scalar is unchanged for each support. If a trivially transforming ancilla is present, keep operator coefficients on that ancilla. Its contribution on each support is their trace pairing times the same scalar, so the equality persists. Classical adaptation over shots preserves equality by the chain rule for conditional probabilities.

With a fourth spin the invariant tensor space on the four-spin support has several independent contractions. Two different invariant singlet pairings let us distinguish the two orders that field reversal exchanges. The [explicit construction](protocol.md) proves that one additional spin-½ suffices.

## The rate bound holds with arbitrary sheltered spins

Fix any feasible field triad and its simultaneous reversal. Write $`K_g=\frac12\sum_i (R_g\mathbf b_i)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_i`$ for the generator when a common rotation $g$ is applied to the fields. It acts trivially on sheltered registers. With $h=3B/2$, it satisfies $\|K_g\|\le h$. Invariant preparation $\rho$ and invariant effects $M_j$ imply that the one-round probabilities equal their Haar average. Their square roots are therefore the $L^2$ norms of the operator-valued functions

```math
a_j^\pm(g)=M_j^{1/2}e^{\mp itK_g}\rho^{1/2},
```

with Hilbert–Schmidt norm in the integrand. For the linearized functions replace the exponential by $`I\mp itK_g`$. Their positive- and negative-sign norms are equal because the Haar average of $K_g$ vanishes. Using $\sum_j M_j=I$, the sum over $j$ of the squared norm of the discarded remainder is at most $t^4 h^4/4$ for either sign. This follows from $`\|e^{ixK}-I-ixK\|\le x^2\|K\|^2/2`$ for a Hermitian $K$.

By the reverse triangle inequality and the triangle inequality on the outcome vectors, the squared distance between the two vectors of square-root probabilities is at most $t^4 h^4$. The one-round affinity, the sum of the products of corresponding square roots, is at least $1-t^4 h^4/2$. The estimate is uniform over joint preparations, effects and numbers of sheltered spins. For a continuous POVM in finite dimensions, take outcome densities with respect to its trace measure and replace the sums by integrals.

For any shared classical history, the conditional next-round experiment obeys the same bound. Recursion of the affinity over $N$ rounds gives transcript affinity at least $`(1-t^4 h^4/2)^N`$. A decision rule with maximum error $\delta$ on this inversion pair has total variation distance at least $1-2\delta$. The affinity and variation inequalities give

```math
(1-t^4 h^4/2)^N\le 2\sqrt{\delta(1-\delta)}.
```

At fixed $B$ and feasible fixed positive chirality gap, this proves $`N=\Omega(t^{-4}\log(1/\delta))`$ as $tB\to0$ for $`0\lt\delta\le1/8`$. It prices the number of fresh rounds and covers classical adaptation. The argument assumes classical information alone is carried between rounds.
